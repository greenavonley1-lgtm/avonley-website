Fitting and Alteration

For this ready-to-wear alterations project, I worked with existing men's and women's garments to improve fit and design through hands-on alteration techniques, including taking in a shirt, hemming pants, and reworking a sleeve into a new silhouette.

Alteration #1 (Men's)

For this dress shirt, I made a few alterations to help it fit better. The biggest issue was that the sleeves were way too big, and there was also a lot of extra fabric through the sides of the shirt. To start, I removed the sleeves from the shirt so I could make the adjustments. I took the sleeves in by a few inches and tapered them so they would fit more comfortably. Next, I pinned the side seams to figure out how much needed to be taken in and sewed the new side seams. Since I changed the shape of the shirt, I also had to redo the hem so it would lay correctly. Because I made the sleeves smaller, I also had to remove the cuffs, shorten them to match the new sleeve width, finish the edges, and sew them back onto the sleeves. After that, I moved the button so the cuff would still button correctly. Once all of the alterations were finished, I completed the seam finishes, reattached the sleeves to the armholes, and finished those seams.

Alteration #2 (Men's)

For this alteration, I hemmed a pair of pants. First, I had him put the pants on so I could see how they fit and mark where I wanted the finished hem to fall. Once I had the correct length marked, I cut off the excess fabric. Then I created a double-fold hem by folding the raw edge up twice, pressing it in place, and sewing around the hem to finish it.

Alteration #3 (Women's)

For this alteration, I hemmed a pair of women's pants. I followed the same process as the previous hem by trying the pants on, marking the desired length, and cutting off the excess fabric. Instead of machine stitching the hem, I finished this pair using a hand-sewn slip stitch.

Alteration #4 (Women's)

For this alteration, the dress sleeve was too tight around my arm, so I decided to make it into a puff sleeve. First, I detached the sleeve. Then, I cut the sleeve to the desired puff sleeve length. Next, I used the excess fabric from the bottom of the sleeve to create a panel matching the design, and attached it to add more ease and fullness. After attaching the panel, I recreated the elastic channel at the bottom of the sleeve where the original cuff had been. I then inserted the elastic and sewed the seam closed. Once that was complete, I gathered the sleeve cap using gathering stitches. Finally, I reattached the sleeve to the armhole.

Pattern Fitting Alterations

In addition to garment alterations, I practiced flat-pattern fitting techniques on a half-scale bodice and sleeve block. These exercises covered common fit adjustments including shoulder, back, bust, and sleeve alterations, helping me understand how to correct fit issues directly on the pattern before cutting fabric.
